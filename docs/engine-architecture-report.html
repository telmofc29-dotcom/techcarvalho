Current architecture

The growth engine is a mature system, not a greenfield. src/lib/engine/ holds roughly sixty modules and sixteen jobs; src/app/admin/(dashboard)/engine/ holds sixteen admin subroutes. It has been running in production since 22 August.

Orchestration is deliberately minimal: Vercel's Hobby plan allows two cron entries, so there is one tick — /api/engine/tick at 04:30 daily — that runs fourteen stages sequentially in dependency order. Each stage is independently flag-gated, independently failure-isolated in its own try/catch, and writes its own engine_job_runs row. Capabilities are added as stages, never as new cron entries.

That stage order is the DISCOVER → RESEARCH → DECIDE → CREATE pipeline the brief asks for, already made literal. Below is the real production state of each stage, measured today.

The single finding that matters

runDraftAssembly consumes only briefs with review_state = 'approved'. Production holds 47 pending and 24 rejected briefs and zero approved ones. Stage 6 has therefore never executed real work, and every capability downstream of an assembled draft has never been exercised in production.

The engine is not failing. It has been correctly producing work for three days that nobody has actioned, because actioning it currently means visiting sixteen separate admin pages and approving items one at a time.

Two safety properties are worth stating plainly because they change what is even possible to get wrong.

Publishing is structurally impossible, not flag-gated. Engine jobs run as anon and reach the database only through narrow SECURITY DEFINER RPCs. engine_assemble_draft is hard-wired to 'draft' and engine_assemble_product to is_published = false. As anon, a direct write setting published state is refused with 42501. The engine cannot publish because there is nothing for it to call. The brief's "never auto-publish" requirement is already enforced by construction.

Autonomy readiness is measured, and it is nowhere near. READINESS requires 500 shadow decisions across 30 distinct days with zero escapes. Production has 195 decisions across 3 distinct days, with 0 human verdicts recorded against them. ASSISTED is the only defensible default, exactly as the brief instructs.

03

Gaps

Four real gaps, in order of how much they cost you today.

1. There is no single review queue, and no concept of "needs attention" anywhere in the codebase. A search across src for any attention/needs-attention concept returns one unrelated comment. The admin dashboard shows plain counts from eight tables and links to nothing under /admin/engine. Work is spread across briefs, discoveries, drafts, freshness, media-acquisition, media-blockers, update-proposals, entity-resolutions, opportunities, trending, searches and homepage. This is the direct cause of the stalled gate.

2. Brief quality is why the queue was ignored. The oldest pending briefs are titles like "NIKKOR Z 50mm f/1.2 S vs NIKKOR Z 50mm f/1.4" carrying 0 verified facts, 0 uncertainties and 0 source URLs. These are combinatorial comparison briefs generated from the internal catalogue, not evidence-backed discoveries. They are correctly unapprovable — and they crowd out the briefs that do carry evidence. A queue whose contents are mostly noise trains its owner to ignore it.

3. Measurement floor. With 48 analytics events in total, demand signals are genuinely unavailable. The engine already handles this honestly: all 12 opportunities carry a null score with the explanation "Not enough measured demand to score (0 combined searches/views, minimum 5)", and 4 of 12 trends report "No measurable signals in this window". That is correct behaviour and must stay. Per your own instruction, it will be surfaced as explicitly unavailable rather than simulated.

4. Dormant subsystems. The knowledge-graph migration created five tables (technology_concepts, product_technologies, content_technologies, technology_relationships, product_claims) that no page, route or action touches — scripts only. engine_change_log exists in SQL with zero TypeScript callers. rollback.ts and modes.ts are display-only. None of these are broken; they are unwired.

04

Proposed engine

Given how much exists, the proposal is deliberately not a new engine. It is one new surface plus four wiring jobs, and it explicitly does not create parallel systems.

The new surface is Today — a single owner review queue at /admin/engine that reads the existing tables and presents every item awaiting a human decision as one ranked list, with the reason it needs you and the action inline. It replaces nothing: the sixteen specialist pages remain as the drill-down, and Today becomes the way in.

Each row is a decision, not a report. A brief row carries its verified facts, uncertainties and source count on its face, so approving is a judgement made from the queue rather than after three clicks. Rows with no evidence are ranked below rows with evidence, which fixes gap 2 without deleting anything.

The approval package turns one approval into the whole downstream sequence: approve brief → assemble draft → attach matched media → propose homepage placement, shown as a preview before you commit and reversible after. It calls the existing RPCs in the existing order; it does not bypass the structural publishing boundary, and the final publish stays a separate, human act.

Target2 actions, 1 page
  1. Review — open Today. The queue is ranked, each row states why it needs you, evidence and rights status visible on the row.
  2. Approve & Publish — one action runs the package: draft assembled, media attached, homepage placement proposed, change logged. Preview first, reversible after.

Anything unsafe — unverified rights, thin sourcing, a possible duplicate — does not enter this path. It is held with the specific reason stated, which is the only thing that should ever cost you more than two actions.

07

Cost impact

No new paid service is required for anything proposed above. The engine is fully deterministic today — ai-provider.ts resolves to a NullAiProvider with no runtime importer, and the only external data source is Wikimedia Commons, rate-limited to one request per 2.5 s. Hosting stays within the Hobby plan's two-cron limit because capabilities are added as tick stages, never as new schedules.

One place where the brief's own constraints collide

Item 22 asks for a real demand-and-opportunity signal. Genuine search-volume data is only sold — Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, Semrush — and item 24 forbids paid services. These cannot both be satisfied.

Recommendation: stay free, and stay honest. Keep first-party signal only, and let opportunity scores remain null with their stated reason until on-site volume can carry them. The engine already does exactly this. I am reporting the paid option here as required, and not enabling it.

The real cost today is your time, and it is being spent on navigation rather than judgement. Phase A targets that directly.
