01

The pipeline, with its missing step

discovery→ relevance→ research→ update proposals→ product assembly→ briefs→ draft assembly→ …

Research runs immediately after relevance — so it only spends fetches on stories already judged worth covering — and before briefs, so a brief is built from whatever corroboration research found rather than from the single feed item that created the discovery.

How it researches without a search API: it fetches the registry's feeds, indexes them in memory, and searches that corpus for the subject. Twenty-three publications' recent output is a real corpus and this is a real search over it — one the project owns rather than rents. Every run records the honest limit: only stories inside those feed windows can be found, and "we found nothing" is distinguished from "we could not have found it".

Why every new source is secondary

That word is load-bearing. primary is what grants first-party single-source sufficiency — Mozilla speaking about Mozilla. An outlet reporting on someone else's product is exactly the case that still needs corroboration, so registering any of these as primary would quietly hand them authority they do not have.

Independence groups, because counting publishers is not counting voices: The Verge and Polygon are both Vox Media. Wired and Ars Technica are both Condé Nast. Tom's Hardware, PC Gamer and Digital Camera World are all Future plc. Three "independent confirmations" that are three Future titles is one voice in three hats.

Declined (not bypassed): VideoCardz, VGC, All3DP, FCC (403 — refused automated access); Ofcom, Wi-Fi Alliance, Photography Blog (404 — feed URLs no longer valid); 3D Printing Industry (415); Notebookcheck (200, no parseable items). A 403 is the publisher saying no, and this project does not rotate user agents around it.

03

Four bugs the live runs caught

Every one of these was mine, and every one would have manufactured corroboration — the single worst thing this pipeline can do.

BugWhat it producedFix
One-word queries score 1.00 Researching "iPhone 18" matched "Apple's four-pack of AirTags is $20 off" and reported six independent origins Queries typed identifying/topical; only identifying ones carry evidence
Query drops its subject anchor "ultra foldable" matched Galaxy S27 Ultra and Xiaomi 18 Fold Identifying queries must retain the subject term
Bag-of-words with no proximity "iPhone" in a download note plus "Ultra" in a watch model = a match Terms must occur within three words of each other
Short tokens discarded "The Witcher 4" became "witcher"; "iPhone 18" became "iphone". Both fell below the query floor and matched nothing — so the pipeline reported "no coverage exists" for stories sitting in the corpus it had just downloaded Numbers kept at any length

The one that worried me most

The fourth. A wrong answer that looks like a principled refusal is worse than a crash, because nothing about it looks broken — and I had already reported "no iPhone 18 coverage exists" on the strength of it. It was only caught because The Witcher 4 was visibly in two feeds I had just read.

Suggested titles are derived from the framing, not from the source headline, so a rumour cannot produce a headline that reads as confirmation: "iPhone 18: what has been reported so far".

Product eligible is No on all three, and that is the point. An article and a catalogue entry have different bars. Rumours about an unreleased phone can justify a well-framed article long before they justify a product page, because a product page asserts that a thing exists with an identity — and specifications are never invented to fill one.

Lineage worked on live data: GSMArena's iPhone story credited Bloomberg and was collapsed, dropping the count from 5 URLs to 4 origins. The Witcher 4 got five genuinely separate newsrooms — The Verge, Engadget, Eurogamer, GameSpot, PC Gamer — with no shared owner between them.

05

Evidence rules, by claim

Claim classOriginsHow it may be written
First-party announcement1Assertable
Third-party report2Attributed
Unreleased-product claim3Attributed
Rumour or leak1Rumour-framed, never assertable

One origin is not automatically a rumour. A reputable outlet reporting its own work is single-source reporting; calling it rumoured misdescribes it and trains a reader to discount the word when it matters. Framing now turns on the language of the claims — a majority of hedged claims makes it rumour coverage whatever the outlet.

Hedging survives extraction. "Apple is reportedly developing a camera system that could arrive in 2027" keeps both hedges. Strip them and you get a fabrication produced entirely by tidying — nobody decided to lie, the hedge just did not survive the pipeline. Hedge beats attribution, and an unrecognised construction defaults to attributed, because failing toward certainty is the dangerous direction.

07

Remaining blockers

  • Evidence cannot persist yet — the migration above. Everything else works.
  • Article body still comes from feed summaries, not full article text. Fetching article pages is the next real gain in claim quality, and needs per-publisher care about what is permitted.
  • Uncited lifts are invisible. If an outlet reproduces another's reporting without saying so, it counts as a second origin. Stated on every assessment rather than papered over.
  • Media automation (Phase H) and the one-package owner view for researched topics are not built. The research output is not yet wired into the approval package.
  • Feed windows are short. A story older than a publication's recent window cannot be found at all.
